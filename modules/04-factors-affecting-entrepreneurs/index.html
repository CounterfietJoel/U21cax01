Factors Affecting Entrepreneurs

Entrepreneurial outcomes emerge from the interaction between personal capability and the environment—not from individual effort alone.

Learning outcome
Identify factors influencing entrepreneurial success.
Estimated time
4–6 minutes

Explore the model

Forces Around the Founder

Use the controls to inspect each idea. Visited controls remain marked during this page visit.

Inputs and demand

Economic factors

Capital, skilled labour, raw materials, infrastructure and market demand determine whether a venture can build and deliver its offer at a sustainable cost.

Norms and support

Social factors

Family support, community values, role models, networks and attitudes toward business affect confidence, access to resources and willingness to choose entrepreneurship.

Motivation and learning

Psychological factors

Need for achievement, self-confidence, persistence, tolerance of uncertainty and willingness to learn shape how a founder interprets setbacks and opportunities.

Rules and enablement

Government and institutional factors

Policy, approvals, taxation, training, incubation, finance and procurement can reduce or increase friction. Apply: Which factor is the current bottleneck for a campus hardware venture, and what evidence supports that judgement?

Contextual case

Connect the concept

Case connection: A technically sound campus product may still stall when testing facilities, early customers, working capital or regulatory guidance are unavailable.

Misconception watch

Do not confuse

Common mistake: Blaming failure on personality alone or assuming a government scheme automatically creates entrepreneurial capability.

Ungraded self-check

Make the decision explicit

Map one proposed venture against the four factor groups and identify the single most limiting constraint.

A useful response should:
  • name the user or stakeholder and the evidence available;
  • separate assumptions from known facts;
  • identify one responsible next action or test.
