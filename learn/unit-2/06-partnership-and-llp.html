II.6 Partnership and Limited Liability Partnership

You should be able to: Compare partnership and LLP forms.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 22
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

A partnership is owned by two or more persons who agree to share profits, responsibilities and risks. It suits founders with complementary skills, but partners usually have unlimited liability. A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a separate legal entity with limited liability and partnership-style flexibility.

  1. Partnership is governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. It is based on an agreement, usually written as a partnership deed. Partners are jointly and severally liable for the firm's debts, and registering the firm is optional but advisable.
  2. LLP is governed by the LLP Act, 2008 and registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. It has a separate legal identity and perpetual succession, each partner's liability is limited to the agreed contribution, and it must make annual filings.
  3. An LLP needs at least two partners, including at least two designated partners. A partnership needs at least two partners.
  4. A founder agreement or partnership deed should cover ownership, roles, decision rights, capital, profit sharing, vesting, IP ownership, confidentiality, exit and dispute resolution.
  5. Friendship is not a substitute for a written agreement.

2Partnership versus LLP

  • PartnershipSimple and flexible, but liability may be high.
  • LLPLimited liability with partnership-style management.
  • Good forProfessional services, consulting, design firms and early ventures with several founders.
  • NeedsA written agreement and clear responsibilities.

3See it applied

Example

An ECE student and a mechanical student forming a small automation consultancy may prefer an LLP once client contracts and liability are involved.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Relying on friendship instead of a written agreement.
  • Leaving ownership and decision rights unclear, which can destroy the venture.
  • Ignoring the liability and tax implications of the chosen form.

5Key terms

Partnership
A relationship between two or more persons who agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all of them or any of them acting for all.
Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
A separate legal entity in which partners manage the business and each partner's liability is limited to the agreed contribution.
Partnership deed
The written agreement setting out capital, profit sharing, roles, decision rights, admission, retirement and dispute resolution.
Founder agreement
A written agreement among co-founders covering equity, roles, decision rights, vesting, IP, confidentiality, exit and disputes.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define partnership.

A partnership is a business owned by two or more persons who agree to share profits, responsibilities and risks under an agreement (the partnership deed). It is governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, and partners usually have unlimited liability.

State the meaning of a Limited Liability Partnership.

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a business form under the LLP Act, 2008 that is a separate legal entity with perpetual succession, where partners manage the business and each partner's liability is limited to the agreed contribution.

List four matters normally covered by a founder agreement.

(1) Equity ownership and vesting, (2) roles and decision rights, (3) intellectual-property ownership, and (4) exit, deadlock and dispute resolution. Capital contribution, salary and confidentiality are also valid.
