II.4 Product planning and development process

You should be able to: Describe stages of product planning and development.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 19
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Product planning turns an opportunity into a product concept, specifications, a prototype, a test plan, a launch plan and an improvement cycle. Development is iterative and stage-gated. Each stage produces evidence and a decision before more money is committed.

  1. A good plan balances desirability (users want it), feasibility (it can be built), viability (it makes financial sense) and responsibility (it avoids harm).
  2. Sequence: define the problem and user → value proposition → assumptions and requirements → concepts → architecture → prototype → test → cost and capacity → MVP → controlled pilot → market-ready release.
  3. A prototype answers a learning question. An MVP is the smallest coherent offering that lets a real customer experience the core value. A pilot tests it in a controlled real setting. A market-ready product adds reliability, quality, support, compliance, supply and cost control.
  4. A product roadmap links outcome, assumption, capability, release and review. Commitment rises as uncertainty falls.
  5. Feature priority depends on customer value, safety and compliance, testing the riskiest assumption, dependencies and delivery cost. It should not be decided by what the team likes.

2Product planning stages

  • Need identificationCustomer analysis and the problem to solve.
  • ConceptGenerate and screen concepts.
  • Specification and designTechnical targets, design, prototype and validation.
  • Costing and sourcingCost, materials, suppliers and manufacturability.
  • Launch and improveRelease, collect feedback and improve.
Prototype, MVP, pilot and market-ready product

3See it applied

Example

A wearable posture monitor must be planned for sensor accuracy, battery life, comfort, app interface, price, data privacy and after-sales support, not just the sensing circuit.

Case lens

Canectar (CaneBOT)

Canectar began with juice kiosks. Market learning about hygiene and convenience led to an automated robotic vending machine, and IP became part of the product logic.

Think: Which stage-gate evidence would justify moving from kiosks to building CaneBOT?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Freezing the design before user testing.
  • Ignoring serviceability and spare parts.
  • Deciding cost targets too late.

5Key terms

Product planning
Converting an opportunity into a concept, specifications, prototype, test plan, launch plan and improvement cycle.
Prototype
An early representation built to answer a defined learning question.
Minimum viable product (MVP)
The smallest coherent offering that delivers core value to a target customer and produces market learning.
Product roadmap
A time-phased plan linking customer outcomes, assumptions, capabilities, releases and review points.
Stage gate
A decision point where evidence decides whether development continues, changes, pauses or stops.

    An MVP is the smallest coherent offering that lets you learn from real customers.

  2. 2What distinguishes a prototype from a finished product?

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

State the meaning of product planning.

Product planning is the process of converting a selected opportunity into a product concept, technical specifications, prototype, test plan, launch plan and improvement cycle, balancing what users want with what can be built profitably and responsibly.

State the meaning of a minimum viable product.

A minimum viable product (MVP) is the smallest coherent version of an offering that lets a target customer experience the core value and gives the venture real market learning with limited effort.

Define a prototype.

A prototype is an early, often incomplete representation of a product built to answer a specific learning question about form, function, usability or performance.
