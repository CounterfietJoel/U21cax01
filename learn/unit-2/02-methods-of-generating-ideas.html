II.2 Methods of generating ideas

You should be able to: Explain methods used for idea generation.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 18
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Idea generation can be systematic. The common methods are brainstorming, mind mapping, SCAMPER, customer interviews, observation studies, problem trees, design thinking, competitor analysis and technology transfer. Generating ideas and selecting them are two separate stages.

  1. Brainstorming asks for quantity before judgement. Criticism is postponed until later.
  2. SCAMPER asks whether you can Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate or Rearrange (reverse) part of an existing product.
  3. Mind mapping visually connects problems, users, causes and possible solutions.
  4. Divergent methods produce alternatives. Convergent screening then compares value, evidence, feasibility, cost, risk, compliance and team fit.
  5. Product selection has three steps (Aurangabadkar and Singh, Fig. 2.3): idea generation → evaluation → choice.

2From many ideas to one

  • DivergeGenerate many possible ideas.
  • ClusterGroup them by user, technology, cost and impact.
  • ScreenRemove ideas with weak need or poor feasibility.
  • ValidateTest assumptions with users and small prototypes.
  • RefineImprove the idea using the evidence.
Product or venture selection criteria (Aurangabadkar and Singh)
CriterionWhat to ask
MarketIs there enough demand, and who will buy?
FundsCan the money needed to develop, produce and sell be raised?
Raw materialsAre inputs available in quantity, quality and at a reachable location?
Technical implicationsCan it be produced with available technology, machines and skills?
ProfitabilityWill it earn a margin and is it marketable?
PersonnelAre qualified people available to produce and sell it?
Government policyDo incentives or rules favour or block it?

3See it applied

Example

To save energy in hostels, students brainstorm motion sensors, dashboards, gamified savings, auto-cutoff systems and awareness campaigns, then choose one feasible approach.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Starting criticism too early in a brainstorm.
  • Generating ideas only within the team and never with users.
  • Assuming a technically exciting idea is commercially strong.

5Key terms

Brainstorming
A group method for generating many ideas quickly by postponing criticism and building on each other's suggestions.
SCAMPER
Seven prompts for transforming an existing product, which are Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate and Reverse or Rearrange.
Divergent thinking
Generating many varied alternatives before applying judgement.
Convergent thinking
Narrowing alternatives by comparing them against evidence and decision criteria.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Name any two methods of generating business ideas.

(1) Brainstorming and (2) SCAMPER. Mind mapping, customer interviews, observation studies, problem trees, design thinking and competitor analysis are also valid.

Define brainstorming.

Brainstorming is a group idea-generation method in which participants produce as many ideas as possible on a clearly stated problem, postpone criticism and build on each other's suggestions. Screening happens in a separate later stage.

Name the three product-selection stages shown in the textbook figure.

Aurangabadkar and Singh (Fig. 2.3): (1) idea generation, (2) evaluation (screening and pre-feasibility study) and (3) choice.

List any two criteria used to select a product or venture.

(1) Market demand and marketability, and (2) technical feasibility with available technology and skills. Availability of funds, raw materials, qualified personnel, profitability and government policy are also valid.
