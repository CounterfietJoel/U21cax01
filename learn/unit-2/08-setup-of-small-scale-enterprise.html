II.8 Setup process of small scale enterprise

You should be able to: Describe the steps for setting up a small-scale enterprise.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 24
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Setting up a small enterprise moves from opportunity and product selection through feasibility, legal form, the project report and finance to location, machinery, staffing, registration, approvals, production and marketing. The project report is the central planning document.

  1. Classic sequence (Charantimath, Fig. 5.1): select the project (product, location, feasibility, business plan and project profile) → decide the constitution (legal form) → register → obtain clearances → arrange land or shed → prepare the project report → arrange finance → arrange plant, machinery, power, water and people → implement.
  2. The project report explains the product, market, technical process, investment, working capital, profitability, risks and implementation schedule.
  3. Start at a realistic scale. Buying fixed assets before market validation creates financial pressure.
  4. Working capital (inventory, wages, utilities and receivables) is often underestimated.
  5. Check licences and local approvals early, because they can delay everything else.

2Five-step summary

  • Select and validateChoose the project and confirm demand.
  • Form and locateChoose the legal form and location.
  • PlanPrepare the project report and financial estimates.
  • ResourceArrange finance, infrastructure, machinery, materials and people.
  • Launch and monitorRegister, obtain approvals, start operations and track performance.

3See it applied

Example

Before buying several printers, a small 3D printing service centre must estimate machine utilisation, filament inventory, maintenance cost, design labour, customer acquisition cost and local competition.

Case lens

Autocracy Machinery

An engineering product range of trenchers, pole diggers and power packs needs supply-chain planning, field service and registration discipline, not just good machines.

Think: Which setup step would you check first before buying fabrication machinery?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Buying machinery before confirming demand.
  • Underestimating working capital.
  • Checking licences and local approvals too late.

5Key terms

Project report
The central planning document that explains product, market, process, investment, working capital, profitability, risk and schedule.
Working capital
Funds tied up in short-cycle operating needs such as inventory, receivables and wages.
Feasibility study
An assessment of whether the market, technical, financial, operational and legal conditions support the project.

6Quick check

Pick an answer to see the explanation. Get all of them right to mark this topic as revised.

  1. 1Which checks should come before a small enterprise buys equipment?

    Demand, process, capacity and cash flow decide what equipment is needed and whether it can be paid for.

  2. 2What is the central planning document for a small enterprise?

    The project report brings product, market, process, investment, profitability and schedule together, and banks use it too.

  3. 3Which cost do new enterprises most often underestimate?

    Inventory, wages, utilities and money owed by customers all lock up cash before sales are collected.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

List any two steps in setting up a small enterprise.

(1) Select the project and confirm demand through a feasibility study, and (2) prepare the project report and arrange finance. Choosing the legal form and location, registering and obtaining approvals, and arranging machinery and staff are also valid.
