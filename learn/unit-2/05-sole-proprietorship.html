II.5 Sole proprietorship

You should be able to: Explain sole proprietorship as a business form.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 21
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and controlled by one person. It is the simplest form to start and manage and suits small trading, services, consulting and early experiments. The owner takes all the profit but also bears unlimited liability, because the business has no legal identity separate from the owner.

  1. There is no separate legal entity. Business debts can be recovered from the owner's personal assets.
  2. No special Act governs its formation. The business only needs the registrations its activity requires (for example GST, shop and establishment or local licences).
  3. Decisions are fast and control is direct, but capital and skills are limited to one person.
  4. Continuity depends on the owner, so the business ends or is disrupted if the owner exits.
  5. Raising equity investment is difficult. Review the form when the business grows, takes on co-founders or carries higher liability.

2Features at a glance

  • OwnershipOne person.
  • ControlDirect and simple.
  • ComplianceRelatively low.
  • LiabilityGenerally unlimited.
  • Best suited forSmall, low-risk, owner-managed businesses.
Choosing a legal form, four factors to compare
FactorQuestion to ask
CapitalHow much money is needed, and will outside investors buy a stake?
LiabilityHow much personal risk can the founders accept?
Governance and controlHow many owners, and how will decisions be made?
Compliance and continuityWhat filings can we manage, and must the business outlive any one founder?

3See it applied

Example

A student offering CAD drafting services to local workshops can begin as a sole proprietor after checking the local registration and tax requirements that apply.

Case lens

Autocracy Machinery

A machinery maker that buys tooling, holds inventory and signs dealer contracts carries real liability. That pushes it away from a sole proprietorship, where the owner is personally liable, towards a limited-liability form.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Underestimating the danger of unlimited liability.
  • Expecting to raise equity investment easily.
  • Forgetting that continuity depends heavily on the owner.

5Key terms

Sole proprietorship
A business owned, controlled and managed by one person who receives all profits and bears unlimited liability.
Unlimited liability
The owner's personal assets can be used to pay business debts.
Separate legal entity
A business recognised in law as distinct from its owners, able to own property, contract and sue in its own name.

6Quick check

Pick an answer to see the explanation. Get all of them right to mark this topic as revised.

  1. 1What condition describes a sole proprietorship?

    One owner, direct control and unlimited liability define a sole proprietorship.

  2. 2Which factors are used when selecting a legal form?

    The legal form decides ownership, liability, control, capital entry and compliance, so those are the criteria.

  3. 3Why is a proprietorship unsuitable for a deep-tech startup seeking investors?

    Investors buy shares in a company. A proprietorship has none, and the founder's personal assets are exposed.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define sole proprietorship.

A sole proprietorship is a business owned, controlled and managed by a single person who receives all profits and bears all risks with unlimited liability. The business has no legal identity separate from its owner.

List four factors used to choose a legal form for a venture.

(1) Capital needs and plans for outside investment, (2) liability exposure, (3) governance, control and number of owners, and (4) compliance burden and continuity. Tax treatment and growth plans are also valid.
