II.1 Sources of new ideas

You should be able to: Identify sources of new business ideas.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 17
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Business ideas come from problems, technological change, customer complaints, market gaps, process inefficiencies, hobbies, research, policy change, demographic change and local resource advantages. The best starting point is usually a real, recurring problem, not a technology looking for a use.

  1. Ask what wastes time, money, energy, material or effort, and what users put up with because nothing better exists.
  2. Observation shows what people actually do. Interviews reveal motives, workarounds and priorities. Operational data shows frequency, delay, cost and defects. Use all three together (triangulation).
  3. Ideas can also come from adapting a successful model to a new geography, customer group or engineering domain.
  4. An idea is a possible solution. A business opportunity is an idea backed by evidence of need, a payer and a feasible way to create value.
  5. A good opportunity is attractive, durable, timely and anchored in something that creates value for the buyer (Aurangabadkar and Singh).

2Where ideas come from

  • Personal experiencePain points you have faced yourself.
  • CustomersComplaints, unmet needs and workarounds.
  • Research and technologyLabs, patents, standards and technical papers.
  • Industry exposureIndustrial visits, internships and field observation.
  • Trends and policySustainability, digital transformation, Industry 4.0 and new regulations.
  • Local advantagesRaw materials, skills or clusters available nearby.

3See it applied

Example

A civil engineering student notices that concrete curing checks on sites are often delayed and develops low-cost IoT monitoring for contractors.

Case lens

Eeki Automation

Eeki saw under-utilised land and demand for pesticide-free vegetables, two idea sources (local resources and a sustainability trend), and combined them into one venture.

Think: Which two sources from the framework does Eeki's idea draw on?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Choosing ideas only because they sound fashionable.
  • Pursuing ideas that have no clear user or payer.
  • Not checking whether the problem is frequent and painful enough.

5Key terms

Business idea
A possible product, service or venture concept that responds to a problem or need.
Business opportunity
A favourable, evidence-backed situation in which a defined customer has a meaningful need and a venture can create and capture value.
Triangulation
Confirming a finding using several independent sources such as observation, interviews and data.

6Quick check

  1. 1Which is the most dependable basis for a durable business idea?

    A recurring, observed customer pain gives the idea a real problem to solve.

  2. 2Why should observation, interviews and data be used together?

    Observation shows behaviour, interviews show motives and data shows frequency and cost. Triangulating them reduces bias.

  3. 3A student adapts a successful Bengaluru tiffin-subscription model for hostel students in Coimbatore. The idea source is

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define a business idea.

A business idea is a possible product, service or venture concept that responds to a customer problem or need. It is a starting point that still has to be tested for need, feasibility and value.

State the meaning of a business opportunity.

A business opportunity is a favourable, evidence-backed situation in which a defined customer has a meaningful need, and a venture can feasibly create and capture value by meeting it. It is an idea plus evidence of need, a payer and feasibility.

List any two sources of business ideas.

(1) Customer complaints and unmet needs, and (2) research outcomes, patents and technical papers. Personal experience, industrial visits and internships, policy and sustainability trends, and local resources are also valid.
