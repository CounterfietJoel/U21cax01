II.7 Corporation and franchising

You should be able to: Explain corporation and franchising models.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 22
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

A company (corporation) is a legal entity separate from its owners. It offers limited liability, perpetual succession and a share structure that suits outside investment and scaling, at the cost of more formal compliance. Franchising lets a franchisee operate under a franchisor's brand, system and standards in return for fees and royalties.

  1. Companies in India are governed by the Companies Act, 2013 and incorporated with the Registrar of Companies (MCA).
  2. A private limited company is the common form for scalable startups. It restricts share transfer, needs at least two members and two directors, and can have at most 200 members. A One Person Company (OPC) allows a single member.
  3. Shares make it easy to bring in investors and employee stock options, which is why investors prefer companies.
  4. In franchising, the franchisor provides brand, system, training and standards, and the franchisee invests, runs the local operation and pays fees and royalties.
  5. The trade-off in franchising is lower entry uncertainty in exchange for fees, restrictions and dependence on the brand.
  6. IP can move to a venture by assignment (ownership transfers), licence (exclusive or non-exclusive), joint venture or spin-off.

3See it applied

Example

A robotics training academy may franchise a proven curriculum model, while a deep-tech product startup chooses a private limited company to be investment-ready.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Signing a franchise without studying fees, royalties, territory and exit terms.
  • Ignoring company compliance such as annual filings.
  • Underestimating brand dependency risk in franchising.

5Key terms

Private limited company
A company under the Companies Act, 2013 with limited liability, restricted share transfer and at most 200 members, commonly used for scalable startups.
Incorporation
The legal formation of a company as a separate legal entity.
Franchising
A contractual model in which a franchisee operates using the franchisor's brand, system and standards in return for fees and royalties.
Licence (IP)
Permission to use IP the owner keeps, on defined terms of field, territory, duration and fee.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

State the meaning of a private limited company.

A private limited company is a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013 that is a separate legal entity with limited liability and perpetual succession. It restricts the transfer of shares and has at most 200 members, and it is the common form for investment-ready startups.

Define franchising.

Franchising is a business arrangement in which a franchisor grants a franchisee the right to operate using its brand, products, systems and support, in return for fees and royalties and compliance with set standards.

Name any two routes for transferring intellectual property to a startup.

(1) Assignment, which transfers ownership, and (2) licensing (exclusive or non-exclusive), which permits use while the owner keeps ownership. A joint venture and a spin-off are also valid.
