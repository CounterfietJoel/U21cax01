II.3 Creative problem solving

You should be able to: Apply basic creative problem-solving approaches.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 18
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Creative problem solving is a disciplined process. You define the real problem, gather information, reframe the challenge, generate alternatives, select a solution, test it and learn from the results. Many startups fail because they solve a symptom instead of the root cause.

  1. Low sales may not be a marketing problem. The cause could be pricing, trust, product fit or distribution.
  2. A good problem statement names the user, the context and the unmet need, with evidence, and does not jump to a solution.
  3. Engineering solutions must fit the human context. A design can work in the lab and still fail in the field because of maintenance, training, environment or user behaviour.
  4. The process alternates between divergent thinking (widening the options) and convergent thinking (narrowing with criteria).
  5. Tools include observation, interviews, journey maps and five whys (exploring) and brainstorming, SCAMPER and analogy (diverging). A must-have screen and weighted criteria help when converging, and prototypes and pilots are used to test.

2Six steps

  • DefineState the problem clearly.
  • GatherCollect information from users and data.
  • Find root causesSeparate symptoms from causes, for example with five whys.
  • GenerateCreate several solution paths.
  • EvaluateCompare cost, feasibility, value and risk.
  • Test and revisePrototype, test and improve.

3See it applied

Example

If farmers reject a soil sensor, the cause may not be accuracy. It could be battery life, the lack of a local-language display, seasonal cash flow or distrust of the recommendations.

Case lens

Krishigati

Designing for marginal farmers meant solving labour shortage, affordability and ease of use together, not just building a capable machine.

Think: Write a one-line problem statement for Krishigati that names the user, context and need.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Jumping to solutions before defining the problem.
  • Relying only on assumptions.
  • Ignoring field conditions.

5Key terms

Root cause
The underlying reason a problem occurs. Fixing it removes the symptom.
Problem statement
An evidence-based description of a target user, the situation, the unmet need and its consequence, written without assuming a solution.
Five whys
Asking "why?" repeatedly, about five times, to move from a symptom to its root cause.
