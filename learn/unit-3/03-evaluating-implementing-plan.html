III.3 Evaluating and implementing a business plan

You should be able to: Explain evaluation and implementation of business plans.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 30
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Evaluation checks whether the plan is desirable, feasible, viable, scalable and responsible. Implementation converts the plan into action through milestones, budgets, roles, procurement, production, sales and monitoring.

  1. Evaluate from several angles: market attractiveness, technical feasibility, financial viability, founder capability, regulatory fit and risk exposure.
  2. Charantimath's feasibility screen (Fig. 5.2): generate ideas → initial screening (is the idea prima facie worthwhile? if not, terminate) → plan the feasibility study → financial analysis → is the project worthwhile? (if not, terminate) → prepare the funding proposal.
  3. The two terminate points stop weak ideas cheaply, before money and commitment escalate.
  4. Implementation needs, for every milestone, an owner, budget, metric, due date, dependency, review rhythm and response rule.
  5. A milestone should describe an evidence-producing result, not an activity.

2Five evaluation lenses

  • Market feasibilityIs there real demand?
  • Technical feasibilityCan we build and deliver reliably?
  • Financial viabilityCan revenue exceed cost sustainably?
  • Operational feasibilityCan the resources be arranged?
  • Risk reviewWhat can go wrong, and what is the response?
Activity versus milestone
Weak (activity)Strong (milestone)
Work on pilot launchComplete 20 paid pilot installations with less than 2 percent critical failure by 30 September within ₹4 lakh (owner, operations lead)

3See it applied

Example

A low-cost water purifier plan should be tested for water quality, filter life, replacement cost, user trust, service model and regulatory claims before launch.

Case lens

CaneBOT

Before installing machines at many locations, Canectar would test footfall, hygiene acceptance, uptime and revenue per machine at a few sites. That is the initial screen before larger commitment.

Think: Write one CaneBOT pilot milestone in owner-budget-metric-date form.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Leaving roles unclear, so nobody owns the outcome.
  • Trusting a profitable-looking plan that has a cash-flow gap.
  • Relying on assumptions when pilot evidence could be collected.

5Key terms

Feasibility study
A structured check of whether a project is technically, commercially and financially workable before major commitment.
Initial screening
A quick check of whether an idea is worth studying further, which terminates weak ideas early.
Milestone
A dated, measurable result with an owner and budget that produces evidence of progress.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain the purpose of initial screening and the two terminate decisions in a project-feasibility study.

Initial screening asks whether the idea is prima facie worthwhile. If not, the project is terminated before money is spent on a detailed study. After the feasibility plan and financial analysis, a second decision asks whether the project is worthwhile. If not, it is terminated before a funding proposal is prepared. Both stop weak projects before commitment escalates (Charantimath, Fig. 5.2).

Use the owner-budget-metric-date format to convert a launch objective into a milestone.

Objective: launch the product. Milestone: the operations lead (owner) completes 20 paid pilot installations (metric) with less than 2 percent critical failure by 30 September (date), within ₹4 lakh (budget).

Explain how an implementation plan converts decisions into action.

It breaks each decision into milestones and assigns each one an owner, a budget, a metric, a due date and its dependencies. Progress is reviewed at a fixed rhythm against the metrics, and a response rule says what corrective action follows when a milestone slips.
