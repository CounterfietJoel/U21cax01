III.7 Business pitch

You should be able to: Explain business pitch structure and purpose.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 35
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

A business pitch is a short, persuasive presentation of the opportunity, solution, market, business model, traction, team, financial need and next steps. Its purpose is not to explain every detail but to create enough confidence and interest that the listener wants to continue, support or decide.

  1. A good pitch is simple, evidence-based and audience-specific. A bank focuses on repayment capacity, an investor on growth and returns, and an incubator on innovation and team capability.
  2. Follow the problem → solution → evidence sequence. Establish the pain first, then the offer, then proof that it works and that people want it.
  3. Start with the customer's problem, not with the technology's specifications.
  4. Traction is the strongest evidence, meaning repeated customer behaviour tied to value such as paid pilots, repeat orders or retention.
  5. Always end with a clear ask.

2Six elements of a pitch

  • ProblemWhat pain exists?
  • SolutionWhat are you offering?
  • MarketWho needs it, and how large is the reachable segment?
  • ModelHow will money be earned?
  • TractionWhat proof exists?
  • AskWhat support is required?

3See it applied

Example

A pitch for an industrial safety wearable should start with accident risk and compliance need, not with sensor specifications.

Case lens

Quixy and WONDRx

Both must explain value quickly to non-technical listeners. Who benefits, how the system fits into the workflow, why now, and what evidence supports adoption.

Think: Write WONDRx's opening line as a problem statement a doctor would recognise.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Overloading slides with text.
  • Hiding weak traction behind buzzwords.
  • Ending without a clear ask.

5Key terms

Pitch
A short, persuasive presentation that seeks the listener's next commitment.
Traction
Evidence that customers value the offering, shown by behaviour such as paid use, repeat purchase or retention.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain the problem-solution-evidence sequence in an investor pitch.

The pitch first establishes a specific, important customer problem, then presents the solution and its value to that customer, and then shows evidence such as pilots, paying users, letters of intent or trial results proving that the solution works and is wanted. Each step builds the listener's confidence before the market, model and ask.
