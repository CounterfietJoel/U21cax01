III.9 The investment pitch and its delivery

You should be able to: Explain pitch delivery to a panel of investors.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 37
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Pitch delivery is the oral and visual communication of the business case. A strong delivery is clear, concise, confident and evidence-backed, and shows command of the problem, customer, numbers and next steps. It is rehearsed but natural.

  1. Open with a sharp problem or user story, and explain the solution in simple language.
  2. Show evidence such as a prototype, pilot, users, revenue, letters of intent or trials.
  3. Explain numbers with their assumptions, and present base, upside and downside cases honestly.
  4. Prepare for questions on market size, customer acquisition, margins, technology risk, competition, founder commitment, use of funds and exit or sustainability. Panels judge the founder's thinking as much as the idea.
  5. Close with a precise next step, for example 'We are raising ₹30 lakh to reach 60 customers. We would welcome a follow-up meeting and can share our pilot data.'

2Delivery checklist

  • OpenA sharp problem or user story.
  • ExplainThe solution in plain language.
  • ProvePrototype, pilot, users or revenue.
  • QuantifyNumbers with assumptions.
  • AskState it clearly.
  • AnswerHandle questions respectfully and directly.

3See it applied

Example

When asked about competition, a good founder says: 'Existing solutions are expensive and city-focused. Our first target is tier-2 workshops that need a lower-cost service model.'

Case lens

WONDRx

A panel will ask whether doctors will change their prescribing habit. A strong answer cites adoption data from a pilot rather than claiming that every doctor will use it.

Think: Draft a two-sentence answer to 'Why will busy doctors adopt WONDRx?'

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Reading slides aloud.
  • Overusing jargon.
  • Attacking competitors unfairly or exaggerating claims.
  • Presenting only an optimistic forecast.

5Key terms

Pitch delivery
How the business case is spoken and shown to a panel, including the answers to questions.
Downside scenario
A forecast using pessimistic but plausible assumptions, shown so that the panel can see the risk.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain why financial assumptions must be disclosed honestly in an investor pitch.

Investors price risk using the assumptions. Hidden or inflated assumptions mislead them, can create legal and reputational damage, and destroy trust when actual results diverge. Disclosing assumptions with base, upside and downside cases shows the founder understands the business and lets both sides agree milestones and terms realistically.
