III.6 Organisational plan

You should be able to: Describe organisational planning for a venture.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 34
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

An organisational plan explains the team, roles, reporting relationships, ownership, advisory support, hiring plan, governance and key processes. Early ventures need clarity more than hierarchy. Founders may wear many hats, but product, finance, sales, operations, compliance and customer support must each have an owner.

  1. Identify skill gaps. A technical founder may need help with sales, accounting, legal compliance or operations.
  2. Fill gaps with co-founders, early hires, advisors, mentors or external professionals such as a chartered accountant.
  3. Plan hiring by stage, for example prototype → pilot production → scale, so cost appears in the cash flow at the right time.
  4. Set policies early for finance, customer data, quality, safety and ethics.
  5. Address founder roles, equity and conflict resolution early, ideally in a founder agreement.

2Contents of an organisational plan

  • FoundersRoles and ownership.
  • Core teamWho is responsible for each critical function.
  • AdvisorsMentors and external professionals.
  • Hiring roadmapWhich roles are added at each stage.
  • PoliciesFinance, data, quality, safety and ethics.
Example team moving from prototype to pilot production
FunctionOwner nowGap or next hire
Product and engineeringFounder AProduction technician at pilot stage
Customer discovery and salesFounder BField sales executive after 10 paying customers
Finance and complianceFounder CExternal chartered accountant from day one
Quality and safetyUnassignedFounder A owns it until a QA hire

3See it applied

Example

A student startup may begin with three founders, one for product, one for customer discovery and sales and one for finance and operations, supported by a faculty mentor.

Case lens

CaneBOT

Moving from kiosks to robotic vending needs new capabilities in automation, field maintenance and site partnerships. The organisational plan must show who owns each one.

Think: Which one capability would you hire for first at CaneBOT, and why?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Building a title-heavy team with unclear work.
  • Postponing founder conflicts.
  • Postponing compliance and accounting indefinitely.

5Key terms

Organisational plan
The part of the business plan that sets out team, roles, ownership, advisors, hiring and governance.
Capability gap
A skill the venture needs but the current team does not have.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain the purpose of an organisational plan.

It shows who will do the work and who is accountable. It sets out founder roles and ownership, core-team responsibilities, advisors, the hiring roadmap by stage and governance policies, and it identifies capability gaps. Investors use it to judge whether the team can execute the plan.
