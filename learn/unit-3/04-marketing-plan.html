III.4 Marketing plan

You should be able to: Describe components of a marketing plan.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 31
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

A marketing plan explains how the venture will understand, reach, persuade, acquire, retain and serve customers. It is not only advertising. It covers segmentation, targeting, positioning, product, price, channel, promotion, the sales process and customer relationships.

  1. Marketing starts before launch. Customer interviews, pilot users, competitor observation and willingness-to-pay tests reduce the risk of building something nobody wants.
  2. Engineering founders must turn technical features into customer benefits such as lower cost, safety, speed, convenience, reliability or compliance.
  3. TAM is total demand within stated customer, geography, need and price boundaries. SAM is the part your offer and model can actually serve. SOM is the share you can realistically win in a stated period.
  4. Estimate bottom-up: reachable qualified customers × conversion × units per customer × repeat frequency × net price. State the source and date of every input.
  5. Five friendly interviews cannot justify a national SOM. They can only improve the next assumption and test.

2Components of a marketing plan

  • SegmentationDivide the market into meaningful groups.
  • TargetingChoose the most attractive first segment.
  • PositioningState why the solution is different and valuable.
  • Marketing mixProduct, price, place and promotion.
  • RetentionService, trust, feedback and repeat value.
Acquisition metrics
MetricFormulaWatch out for
Lead-to-customer conversionNew customers / qualified leadsDefine a qualified lead consistently
Customer acquisition cost (CAC)Sales and marketing cost / new customersInclude people, channel, promotion and onboarding cost
RetentionCustomers still active / customers eligible to remainUse a period that fits the model
Repeat purchase rateCustomers buying again / customers eligible to buy againUseful for products and periodic services
CAC paybackCAC / monthly contribution per customerShorter is safer, but depends on contribution and churn

3See it applied

Example

For a solar dryer, the first target may be farmer producer organisations rather than individual farmers, because they can afford it, use it more and decide collectively.

Case lens

Quixy

Quixy sells a capability to business users. Its plan must separate the target segment, the decision-maker who signs, the user who builds the apps, the channel, and the proof of productivity.

Think: For Quixy, who is the user and who is likely the decision-maker in a mid-sized firm?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Defining the target market as everyone.
  • Treating social-media posting as a complete marketing plan.
  • Setting a price without considering value, cost and affordability.

5Key terms

Positioning
The clear statement of why the offering is different and valuable to the target segment.
TAM, SAM, SOM
Total addressable market, serviceable available market and serviceable obtainable market, narrowing from all possible demand to the share realistically won.
CAC
Customer acquisition cost, the sales and marketing spend divided by the number of new customers in the period.

    A segment is specific enough to reach, price and serve.

  2. 2SAM differs from TAM because SAM is

    SAM limits TAM by language, channel, regulation, technology and geography.

  3. 3A venture spends ₹2,00,000 on sales and marketing and gains 40 customers. CAC is

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain the main components of a marketing plan.

Segmentation divides the market into meaningful groups. Targeting picks the first segment. Positioning states why the offer is different and valuable. The marketing mix sets product, price, place and promotion. The sales process and retention cover acquisition, service, trust and repeat value. Metrics such as conversion and CAC track progress.

Use a customer-segment statement to specify two suitable acquisition metrics.

Segment: private dental clinics in Coimbatore with two or more chairs. Metric 1: lead-to-customer conversion, which is clinics signed divided by qualified clinic leads. Metric 2: CAC, which is sales and demo cost divided by clinics signed in the quarter.

Rewrite the claim 'everyone will buy our product' as a testable, evidence-based statement.

For example: 'At least 15 of 60 qualified hostel residents contacted on our campus will pre-order the product at ₹499 within four weeks.' It names a segment, a price, a measurable conversion and a time limit, so it can be tested and proved wrong.
