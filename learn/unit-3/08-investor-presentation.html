III.8 Preparing an investor presentation

You should be able to: Identify elements of an investor presentation.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 36
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

An investor presentation (pitch deck) usually contains the problem, solution, product, market size, business model, competition, go-to-market strategy, traction, team, financials, the funding ask and the use of funds. One slide carries one main idea. Charts, screenshots, customer evidence and simple diagrams beat paragraphs.

  1. Investors look for a meaningful problem, a differentiated solution, a capable team, market evidence, a scalable model, credible financial logic and honesty about risk.
  2. A clear ask states the amount, the instrument where relevant, the use of funds, the milestone it reaches, the metric that proves it and the effect on runway.
  3. Never claim there is no competition. Show alternatives, including doing nothing, and why you are better for the first segment.
  4. Projections need visible assumptions and unit economics, not just a hockey-stick chart.
  5. Sector-specific items matter. For example, a medical device deck needs a regulatory pathway and a clinical validation plan.

2A ten-slide deck

  • 1. TitleVenture name and a one-line value proposition.
  • 2. ProblemCustomer pain with evidence.
  • 3. SolutionProduct and demonstration.
  • 4. MarketTarget segment and TAM, SAM and SOM.
  • 5. Business modelPricing and revenue streams.
  • 6. TractionValidation and early customers.
  • 7. CompetitionAlternatives and your advantage.
  • 8. TeamWhy this team can execute.
  • 9. Financials and milestonesKey numbers with assumptions.
  • 10. AskAmount, use of funds, milestone and runway.

3See it applied

Example

A medical device pitch must include its regulatory pathway and clinical validation plan, not only market size.

Case lens

Quixy

A Quixy deck would show screenshots of an app built in hours, a before-and-after time saving at a client, and the pricing model, rather than paragraphs about no-code technology.

Think: Which single chart would most convince an investor about Quixy's traction?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Claiming there is no competition.
  • Showing hockey-stick projections without assumptions.
  • Ignoring unit economics.

5Key terms

Pitch deck
The set of slides that supports an investor presentation.
Use of funds
The breakdown of how the requested money will be spent and which milestones it will achieve.
Unit economics
Revenue and cost per unit or per customer, such as contribution, CAC and payback.

6Quick check

Pick an answer to see the explanation. Get all of them right to mark this topic as revised.

  1. 1A credible investor ask should state

    Investors fund milestones. The ask links money to measurable progress.

  2. 2A slide says 'We have no competition.' An investor is most likely to read this as

    Customers always have alternatives, even if it is doing nothing.

  3. 3Each slide in a deck should carry

    The deck supports the speaker. One idea per slide keeps it clear.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain what a clear investor ask should contain.

The amount required, the instrument where relevant (equity, convertible note or debt), the use of funds broken into heads, the milestone each use will achieve, the metric that will prove it and the effect on runway, for example ₹30 lakh for 12 months to reach 60 paying customers.
