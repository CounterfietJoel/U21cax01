V.2 Incentives, subsidies, grants and schemes

You should be able to: Describe incentives, subsidies, grants and schemes.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 55
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

These are policy tools that encourage entrepreneurship in priority sectors, regions, social groups and technologies. An incentive encourages a defined behaviour or investment. A subsidy reduces an eligible cost under specified rules. A grant is normally non-repayable if conditions, milestones, permitted use and reporting are met. A scheme is the programme that packages these with eligibility, process and obligations.

  1. Contrast them with other money. A loan creates repayment and often security obligations. Equity exchanges ownership and governance rights for risk capital. A grant is non-dilutive, but it is competitive, documented and purpose-specific.
  2. Misusing a grant or reporting it poorly can lead to recovery of the money or disqualification.
  3. Read the guidelines for eligibility, promoter contribution, project-cost limits, permitted and excluded expenses, reporting or repayment conditions and deadlines.
  4. Check whether support is reimbursed after you spend or given in advance, since that changes your cash flow.
  5. Screen schemes by need and stage first, not by the advertised amount. If a critical condition is unclear, treat the scheme as unverified.

2Types of support

  • Capital subsidySupport for fixed investment.
  • Interest subsidyReduces borrowing cost.
  • Seed grantEarly support for prototype or validation.
  • Training grantSkill and capacity building.
  • Market supportExhibitions, procurement and export promotion.
  • Inclusive schemesFor women, SC/ST, rural, differently abled and first-generation founders.

3See it applied

Example

A startup developing a prototype may need a seed grant, while a small manufacturing unit may benefit more from a machinery subsidy or a credit guarantee.

Case lens

TN Startup and Innovation Policy 2023

Students should separate each scheme's eligibility, benefit, implementing department, application route and limitations, rather than quoting a headline amount.

Think: For any one scheme you know, write its permitted use and one obligation.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Depending fully on subsidies for viability.
  • Not checking whether support is reimbursement after spending or an advance.
  • Not keeping invoices, bank statements and utilisation records.

5Key terms

Incentive
A policy measure that encourages a defined investment or behaviour, such as setting up in a backward region.
Subsidy
Support that reduces a specified eligible cost, such as capital or interest, under scheme rules.
Grant
Funding that is normally non-repayable if its conditions, permitted use and reporting are met.
Permitted use
The specific expenses on which scheme money may lawfully be spent.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define an entrepreneurial incentive.

An entrepreneurial incentive is a government policy measure, such as a tax concession, subsidy or preferential access, that encourages a defined investment or behaviour, for example setting up in a priority sector or region.

Explain the difference between a subsidy and a grant.

A subsidy reduces a specified eligible cost, such as capital investment or interest, usually as a percentage of that cost and often as reimbursement. A grant is a sum given for a defined purpose, such as prototype development, that is normally not repayable if milestones, permitted use and reporting conditions are met.

Explain the difference between a grant and equity investment.

A grant is non-repayable and non-dilutive, with eligibility, permitted-use and reporting obligations. Equity investment gives money in exchange for ownership, so founders are diluted and the investor gains governance rights and expects a return through growth and exit.

Explain the meaning of scheme eligibility and permitted use of funds.

Eligibility is the set of conditions an applicant must meet, such as entity type, age, turnover, sector, location and stage. Permitted use is the list of expenses on which the scheme money may be spent. Spending on anything else can trigger recovery.
