Unit V · CO5 · Make use of Government initiatives and support organisations for starting new ventures

Government Initiatives and Institutional Support

Support is stage-specific capital and capability, not an entitlement. This unit covers what Central and State governments do, the difference between incentives, subsidies, grants and schemes, inclusive growth, Startup India, the MSME Act and Udyam, Tamil Nadu support channels, and every major source of finance from banks to angels, VC, bootstrapping, crowdfunding and alliances.

Hold on to two rules. Match the instrument to the stage and the evidence: founder money and grants for learning, debt when repayment is visible, equity for scalable uncertainty. Verify on the official portal: scheme names, limits and windows change, so an unclear condition means the scheme is unverified.

2-mark questions with model answers

Write your answer first. A 2-mark answer needs a precise definition or two clear points, not a paragraph.

List any four forms of government support for entrepreneurship.

(1) Policy and recognition, (2) infrastructure such as industrial estates, (3) finance such as loans, subsidies, guarantees and grants, and (4) capacity building through EDPs, training and mentoring. Market access through public procurement and GeM is also valid.

State the role of a District Industries Centre.

A District Industries Centre is the district-level government office that guides entrepreneurs, processes selected enterprise schemes and facilitates setup. Its mandate and scheme coverage must be checked locally.

Define an entrepreneurial incentive.

An entrepreneurial incentive is a government policy measure, such as a tax concession, subsidy or preferential access, that encourages a defined investment or behaviour, for example setting up in a priority sector or region.

Explain the difference between a subsidy and a grant.

A subsidy reduces a specified eligible cost, such as capital investment or interest, usually as a percentage of that cost and often as reimbursement. A grant is a sum given for a defined purpose, such as prototype development, that is normally not repayable if milestones, permitted use and reporting conditions are met.

Explain the difference between a grant and equity investment.

A grant is non-repayable and non-dilutive, with eligibility, permitted-use and reporting obligations. Equity investment gives money in exchange for ownership, so founders are diluted and the investor gains governance rights and expects a return through growth and exit.

Explain the meaning of scheme eligibility and permitted use of funds.

Eligibility is the set of conditions an applicant must meet, such as entity type, age, turnover, sector, location and stage. Permitted use is the list of expenses on which the scheme money may be spent. Spending on anything else can trigger recovery.

Explain the meaning of inclusive entrepreneurial growth.

Inclusive entrepreneurial growth means widening the opportunity to start and grow ventures to under-served groups and regions, such as women, rural youth, first-generation, marginalised and differently abled founders, through access to finance, skills, networks, infrastructure and markets, with measurable outcomes.

Explain what Startup India recognition may enable and what it does not guarantee.

Recognition may enable access to self-certification, startup IPR support, Section 80-IAC tax exemption (subject to conditions), easier public-procurement norms, easier winding up and selected funding channels. It does not guarantee investment, a bank loan, tax relief, procurement orders, sector approvals, customers or commercial success.

Distinguish DPIIT startup recognition from Udyam MSME registration.

DPIIT recognition is a startup status under Startup India, based on age, turnover, eligible form and innovation, and it opens startup-specific benefits. Udyam registration is MSME identity and classification based on investment and turnover, and it opens MSME benefits such as credit, procurement and delayed-payment protection. Neither is incorporation, and an enterprise may hold both.

Name any two Tamil Nadu support channels for entrepreneurs.

(1) StartupTN and its TANSEED and ecosystem programmes for innovative early-stage startups, and (2) NEEDS for eligible first-generation entrepreneurs. TWEES and UYEGP are also valid. Current conditions must be verified on official portals.

State why scheme details must be reverified before applying.

Scheme names, amounts, eligibility, windows and obligations change between cohorts and policy revisions, so only the current official portal is a reliable basis for an application.

List four common sources of startup finance.

(1) Owner funds or bootstrapping, (2) bank loans, (3) government grants and seed funds, and (4) angel investment. Venture capital, crowdfunding, customer advances and supplier credit are also valid answers.

Explain the difference between a term loan and working-capital finance.

A term loan finances long-lived assets such as machinery or buildings and is repaid in instalments over several years. Working-capital finance, such as cash credit or an overdraft, funds the operating cycle, meaning inventory, receivables, wages and utilities, and is drawn and repaid as cash moves.

List four items normally examined during bank loan appraisal.

(1) Promoter background and contribution, (2) project cost and technical feasibility, (3) projected cash flow and repayment capacity, and (4) collateral or guarantee and credit history.

Explain the meaning of repayment capacity.

Repayment capacity is a borrower's credible ability to pay loan principal and interest on schedule from projected cash flows, after meeting operating costs and working-capital needs. Collateral reduces the lender's loss but does not create repayment capacity.

Define cash runway and state the information required to estimate it.

Cash runway is the number of months a venture can operate before cash runs out, estimated as available cash ÷ monthly net burn. It needs the current cash balance, expected monthly receipts and payments (net burn) and known one-time costs such as equipment, tax or debt service.

Explain the difference between angel investment and venture capital.

Angels are individuals investing their own money, usually smaller amounts at an early stage, often with mentoring. Venture capital firms invest larger pooled funds at later stages in startups with proven high-growth potential, taking equity, board or governance rights and expecting a large return through exit.

Explain bootstrapping with one example.

Bootstrapping is building a venture with founder savings, customer revenue and advances and careful cost control instead of outside equity. For example, a CAD-drafting service starts from a founder's laptop, takes 50 percent advances from clients and reinvests profits to hire its second designer.

Explain the difference between debt finance and equity finance.

Debt finance is borrowed money that must be repaid with interest on a schedule, often with security, while ownership stays with the founders. Equity finance is money given for a share of ownership. It has no fixed repayment, but it dilutes the founders and gives investors governance rights and a share of future value.

Explain how a government-backed Fund of Funds normally operates.

The government commits capital to selected SEBI-registered Alternative Investment Funds rather than to startups directly. Those funds add private capital, appraise startups and invest in them on commercial terms, so public money is multiplied and professionally managed.

Outline a stage-wise sequence of finance from prototype to production.

At prototype stage, founder funds, incubation support and seed grants. At pilot stage, angel or seed equity and customer advances, backed by traction. At setup and production, promoter contribution plus a bank term loan for machinery and a working-capital limit. At scale, venture capital or venture debt. Each step funds the evidence that justifies the next.

Explain reward-based and equity crowdfunding.

In reward-based crowdfunding, contributors receive a product, service or other reward, so the founder must deliver honestly and on time. In equity crowdfunding, contributors receive ownership, which brings valuation, disclosure, governance and securities-law duties that must be verified before use.

Explain the purpose of a strategic alliance.

A strategic alliance lets a startup access complementary resources it lacks, such as technology, manufacturing, distribution, customers or credibility, from a partner without merging or full ownership. It is governed by a written agreement on contributions, IP, quality, customer ownership and exit.

State two ethical duties in a crowdfunding campaign.

(1) Disclose honestly what is proven and what is uncertain, including delivery risk. (2) Use funds only for the stated purpose and report progress regularly, including bad news, with a predefined refund or remedy if the promise cannot be met.

14-mark questions

Plan before you write: an introduction, the points below as headings with explanation and an example, then a short conclusion. Open a question to see the points to cover.

1Apply a stage-wise finance framework to match promoter contribution, incubation, grants, bank finance and equity with a student manufacturing startup.

Points to cover

  • Idea and prototype — promoter contribution, incubator support and lab access.
  • Validation — a seed grant tied to milestones and permitted use.
  • Pilot — customer advances, plus angel or seed equity if growth potential is high.
  • Setup — promoter margin, a term loan for machinery and a working-capital limit for inventory and receivables.
  • Scale — VC or venture debt once unit economics are proven.
  • Each stage funds the evidence for the next, with runway checked at each step.

Study: V.7 Entrepreneurial finance and commercial banks, V.8 Venture capital, angel investors and bootstrapping

2Explain the roles and limitations of Central and State Government institutions, MSME bodies, banks, incubators and investors in promoting entrepreneurship.

Points to cover

  • Central bodies (Ministry of MSME, DPIIT) provide policy, recognition and programmes, but guarantee no finance or customers.
  • State and district bodies (DIC, StartupTN) provide facilitation and schemes, with local mandates and competitive selection.
  • MSME bodies (NSIC and others) provide market, procurement and raw-material support, with conditions.
  • Banks and SIDBI provide debt and refinance, but need repayment logic and documents.
  • Incubators and universities provide mentoring, labs and seed support, with IP and liability agreements.
  • Angels and VCs provide risk capital and networks, but expect ownership, scale and exit. Approach each by its mandate.

Study: V.1 Role of Central and State Governments

3Use an application-readiness checklist to organise eligibility, evidence, documents, permitted use, milestones, reporting and current-source verification for a support scheme.

Points to cover

  • Administering body and objective.
  • Eligibility — entity, age, turnover, sector, location and stage.
  • Evidence and documents — deck, registration, founder profile and financials.
  • Contribution and permitted and excluded use.
  • Milestones, reporting and the consequence of non-compliance.
  • Official source and date checked. Any unclear condition means the scheme is unverified.

4Explain the purposes, benefits, limitations and practical uses of DPIIT startup recognition and Udyam MSME registration.

Points to cover

  • DPIIT purpose — startup status, with eligibility on age, turnover, form, innovation and no reconstruction.
  • DPIIT benefits — self-certification, IPR support, 80-IAC (conditional), procurement and winding up.
  • Udyam purpose — MSME identity and classification by investment and turnover (micro, small and medium limits).
  • Udyam benefits — credit linkage, procurement and MSMED Act delayed-payment protection (45 days, interest at 3× bank rate).
  • Limitations — neither is incorporation, a licence or funding, and neither guarantees customers.
  • Practical use — a manufacturing startup may hold both, verified on the official portals.

5Apply fixed-asset, working-capital, promoter-contribution, debt-service and cash-flow categories to the financing needs of a small fabrication unit.

Points to cover

  • Fixed assets — welding sets, press, CNC and installation, financed by a term loan plus promoter margin.
  • Working capital — steel inventory, receivables and wages, financed by a cash-credit limit.
  • Promoter contribution — the founder's share of project cost, showing commitment.
  • Debt service — EMI schedule and interest, set against cash flow.
  • Cash flow — monthly receipts and payments, collection delay and a buffer.
  • Repayment capacity and the downside case support the bank appraisal.

6Use stage, evidence, repayment, ownership and control criteria to select suitable finance from bootstrapping, grants, debt, angel investment and venture capital.

Points to cover

  • Build a criteria table with stage, evidence, repayment, ownership and control as rows.
  • Bootstrapping keeps full control with no repayment, but is slow.
  • Grants are non-dilutive, but carry eligibility and reporting duties.
  • Debt preserves ownership, but needs cash flow and security.
  • Angels bring early equity and mentoring with some dilution. VC brings large equity for proven scale, with governance rights.
  • Apply this to a stated venture and justify the choice.

7Apply a crowdfunding checklist covering campaign type, target, evidence, communication, fulfilment, fund use, risk disclosure and legal verification to a campus-product case.

Points to cover

  • Type — reward-based, which suits a campus product.
  • Target — costed production, tax, platform fees, shipping and a contingency for delays.
  • Evidence — a working prototype and supplier quotes.
  • Communication and fulfilment — milestones, regular updates and a delivery plan.
  • Fund use and risk disclosure — what is proven, what is uncertain and the refund policy.
  • Legal — platform terms and consumer, tax and contract duties verified.

8Use contribution, governance, IP, quality, performance and exit headings to outline a strategic alliance among a startup, manufacturer, distributor and technical institution.

Points to cover

  • Contribution — design (startup), production (manufacturer), channel (distributor), testing (institution).
  • Governance — steering committee, decision rights and dispute resolution.
  • IP — background IP and new IP ownership, licences and confidentiality.
  • Quality — specifications, inspection and corrective action.
  • Performance — volumes, lead times, service levels and payment terms.
  • Exit — duration, termination triggers, IP and customer-data handover.

Study: V.2 Incentives, subsidies, grants and schemes, V.6 Tamil Nadu startup schemes

10(i) Explain the differences between bootstrapping and angel investment.(ii) Use stage and runway criteria to choose between them for a prototype-stage venture.

Points to cover

  • Bootstrapping — own funds and revenue, full control, slower growth.
  • Angel — outside equity, mentoring and networks, with dilution.
  • At prototype stage evidence is low, so angels may be hard to win and valuation low.
  • Compute runway = cash ÷ burn. If it covers the next validation milestone, bootstrap.
  • If the milestone needs more cash than the runway allows and growth potential is high, seek an angel.
  • Conclude with a stated choice and its reason.

11(i) Use a bank-proposal template to organise promoter contribution, asset cost, working capital, cash flow, repayment and risk-control information.(ii) Demonstrate how the organised information supports loan appraisal.

Points to cover

  • Blocks — promoter and entity, purpose, project cost, sources of funds.
  • Market and operations evidence, permissions and security.
  • Financial projection with monthly cash flow, break-even and sensitivity.
  • Debt-service schedule, repayment capacity and downside case.
  • Appraisal link — contribution shows commitment, cash flow shows capacity, security limits loss.
  • Risk controls and monitoring build lender confidence.

Study: V.7 Entrepreneurial finance and commercial banks

12(i) Apply an ethics checklist to a crowdfunding campaign that overstates readiness and conceals delivery risk.(ii) Use transparent communication, milestone, refund and reporting practices to correct it.

Points to cover

  • Overstating readiness misleads contributors, and concealed risk breaks trust and may breach consumer law.
  • Checklist items — proven versus uncertain, full costing, realistic schedule and fund use.
  • Correction — restate readiness honestly with the prototype status.
  • Publish milestones and regular updates, including delays.
  • Define a refund, substitution or remedy policy in advance.
  • Keep records and do not divert funds without disclosure and consent.

Study: V.9 Crowdfunding and strategic alliances

13(i) Use ownership, governance, repayment and cash-runway criteria to select debt, equity or a combination for a stated pilot-production need.(ii) Illustrate how the selected mix fits the venture's stage and use of funds.

Points to cover

  • Debt preserves ownership, but needs repayment from cash flow.
  • Equity brings dilution and governance, with no repayment, and suits uncertainty.
  • Runway — equity extends it without EMIs, while debt adds an obligation.
  • A likely mix is equity or a grant for uncertain pilot work, and debt for tooling with predictable use.
  • Map each use of funds to its source and milestone.
  • Explain why the mix fits the pilot stage.

Study: V.8 Venture capital, angel investors and bootstrapping, V.7 Entrepreneurial finance and commercial banks

14(i) Use need and stage categories to match a student hardware startup with appropriate Tamil Nadu and national support channels.(ii) List the details that must be reverified from official portals before application.

Points to cover

  • Prototype — campus incubator and lab.
  • Ecosystem and seed — StartupTN and TANSEED calls.
  • Recognition — DPIIT if eligible, and Udyam after setup.
  • Enterprise schemes — NEEDS, TWEES or UYEGP only if the applicant conditions fit. PMEGP for micro units.
  • Finance — bank or equity once evidence justifies it.
  • Reverify the call window, eligibility, amount, contribution, equity stake, permitted use, milestones and source date.

Study: V.6 Tamil Nadu startup schemes, V.4 Startup India scheme

15(i) Apply a source-of-funds reporting checklist to grant, loan and equity money used in the same venture.(ii) Demonstrate how records and controls differ according to repayment, ownership and reporting obligations.

Points to cover

  • Grant — permitted use, utilisation certificates, milestone reports and invoices, with risk of recovery.
  • Loan — end-use as sanctioned, EMI schedule, stock statements and covenants.
  • Equity — shareholder records, board reporting and investor information rights.
  • Track each source separately in the books (separate cost centres or accounts where required).
  • Controls — approvals, reconciliation and audit trail.
  • Mixing sources without records risks recovery, default or investor disputes.
