V.7 Entrepreneurial finance and commercial banks

You should be able to: Identify sources of entrepreneurial finance and bank support.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 61
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Sources of entrepreneurial finance include owner funds, family and friends, bank loans, government schemes, microfinance, grants, seed funds, angels, venture capital, crowdfunding, supplier credit, customer advances and retained earnings. Commercial banks finance MSMEs and traditional enterprises after appraising viability, promoter contribution, collateral or guarantee, cash flow, credit history and repayment capacity.

  1. A term loan finances long-lived assets such as machinery and buildings, repaid over years. Working-capital finance (cash credit or overdraft) funds inventory, receivables, wages and utilities through the operating cycle.
  2. Repayment capacity is the credible ability to pay principal and interest from projected cash flows after operating and working-capital needs. Security reduces lender loss but does not fix an unviable cash flow.
  3. Banks appraise the promoter, purpose, contribution, project cost, market, technical feasibility, permissions, collateral, cash flow, debt service, risk and records.
  4. Cash runway = available cash ÷ monthly net burn. It needs the cash balance, expected receipts and payments, and one-time costs. It is an indicator, not a guarantee.
  5. Loans must be repaid even if sales are delayed. Do not divert working capital to unrelated spending.

2Bank finance building blocks

  • Fixed capitalMachinery, building, equipment and tools.
  • Working capitalInventory, receivables, wages and utilities.
  • Term loanLong-term asset finance.
  • Cash credit or overdraftWorking-capital support.

3See it applied

Example

A small fabrication unit may use promoter contribution plus a term loan for machines, and a working-capital limit for raw material and receivables.

Case lens

SIDBI seed funding and venture debt

SIDBI-linked seed money reaches early startups through incubators, while venture debt suits later startups that already have investors and revenue. The same institution offers different instruments at different stages.

Think: Why would a bank prefer to lend against a machine rather than fund early prototype experiments?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Forgetting that loans must be repaid even if sales are delayed.
  • Using working capital for unrelated expenses.
  • Approaching banks without records and financial discipline.

5Key terms

Term loan
A loan for long-lived assets, repaid in instalments over several years.
Working-capital finance
Short-term finance, such as cash credit or an overdraft, for inventory, receivables and operating needs.
Repayment capacity
The ability to meet principal and interest from projected cash flow after operating needs.
Promoter contribution
The founders' own money invested in the project, which lenders expect as a share of project cost.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

List four common sources of startup finance.

(1) Owner funds or bootstrapping, (2) bank loans, (3) government grants and seed funds, and (4) angel investment. Venture capital, crowdfunding, customer advances and supplier credit are also valid answers.

Explain the difference between a term loan and working-capital finance.

A term loan finances long-lived assets such as machinery or buildings and is repaid in instalments over several years. Working-capital finance, such as cash credit or an overdraft, funds the operating cycle, meaning inventory, receivables, wages and utilities, and is drawn and repaid as cash moves.

List four items normally examined during bank loan appraisal.

(1) Promoter background and contribution, (2) project cost and technical feasibility, (3) projected cash flow and repayment capacity, and (4) collateral or guarantee and credit history.

Explain the meaning of repayment capacity.

Repayment capacity is a borrower's credible ability to pay loan principal and interest on schedule from projected cash flows, after meeting operating costs and working-capital needs. Collateral reduces the lender's loss but does not create repayment capacity.

Define cash runway and state the information required to estimate it.

Cash runway is the number of months a venture can operate before cash runs out, estimated as available cash ÷ monthly net burn. It needs the current cash balance, expected monthly receipts and payments (net burn) and known one-time costs such as equipment, tax or debt service.
