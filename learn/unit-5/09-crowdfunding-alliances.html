V.9 Crowdfunding and strategic alliances

You should be able to: Explain crowdfunding and strategic alliances for startups.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 63
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Crowdfunding raises small contributions from many people, usually online. It may be donation-, reward-, debt- or equity-based, depending on law and platform rules. A strategic alliance is a partnership with another organisation to access technology, customers, distribution, manufacturing, credibility or complementary resources without full ownership or merger. Both depend on trust, backed by clear communication and written agreements.

  1. Reward-based contributors get a product or reward. The founder's duty is honest readiness, fulfilment, communication and a remedy. Its main failure is an under-costed reward or late or no delivery.
  2. Equity-based contributors get ownership, which brings valuation, disclosure, governance and securities-law consequences. Verify the current legal position in India before use.
  3. Campaign ethics require you to state what is proven and what is uncertain, cost production, tax, platform fees and delays, set milestones, report bad news as well as good, define refunds in advance and never divert funds without disclosure.
  4. An alliance agreement defines contributions, decision rights, IP, confidentiality, quality, customer ownership, performance, financial terms, conflict handling, duration and exit.
  5. Alliances should protect IP and customer data. Dependence on one partner is a risk.

2Types

  • CrowdfundingCommunity funding, pre-orders and public validation.
  • Technology allianceR&D or product integration.
  • Market allianceChannel or distribution partner.
  • Manufacturing allianceContract production without owning the plant.
  • Institutional allianceCollege, incubator, industry association, NGO or government body.

3See it applied

Example

A sustainable campus product can use reward-based crowdfunding for early users and partner with a local manufacturer for production.

Case lens

SIDBI, Fund of Funds and alliances

A startup, a contract manufacturer, a distributor and a technical institute can form an alliance without merging. The written agreement on IP, quality, customer ownership and exit is what makes it trustworthy.

Think: In such an alliance, who should own customer data, and why must the agreement say so?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Making crowdfunding promises that cannot be kept.
  • Leaving IP and customer data unprotected in alliances.
  • Becoming dependent on a single partner.

5Key terms

Crowdfunding
Raising small amounts from many people, usually through an online platform.
Reward-based crowdfunding
Contributors receive a product or other non-ownership reward for their support.
Equity crowdfunding
Contributors receive an ownership interest, with legal and governance consequences.
Strategic alliance
A formal partnership that shares complementary resources without full merger or ownership.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain reward-based and equity crowdfunding.

In reward-based crowdfunding, contributors receive a product, service or other reward, so the founder must deliver honestly and on time. In equity crowdfunding, contributors receive ownership, which brings valuation, disclosure, governance and securities-law duties that must be verified before use.

Explain the purpose of a strategic alliance.

A strategic alliance lets a startup access complementary resources it lacks, such as technology, manufacturing, distribution, customers or credibility, from a partner without merging or full ownership. It is governed by a written agreement on contributions, IP, quality, customer ownership and exit.

State two ethical duties in a crowdfunding campaign.

(1) Disclose honestly what is proven and what is uncertain, including delivery risk. (2) Use funds only for the stated purpose and report progress regularly, including bad news, with a predefined refund or remedy if the promise cannot be met.
