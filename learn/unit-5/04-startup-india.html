V.4 Startup India scheme

You should be able to: Explain the Startup India scheme and benefits.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 57
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Startup India is a national initiative supporting eligible startups through DPIIT recognition, easier compliance, IPR support, tax-related benefits, public-procurement relaxation, funding links and ecosystem access, subject to current rules. Recognition is useful, but it does not guarantee funding or customers.

  1. Eligibility (Course Reader note, verified 2 August 2026, recheck before use): a private limited company, registered partnership, LLP or cooperative society, up to 10 years old with turnover below ₹200 crore, or 20 years and ₹300 crore for DeepTech. It must work towards innovation or improvement with employment or wealth-creation potential, and must not be formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business.
  2. Benefits listed on the official page include self-certification, startup patent and IPR support, Section 80-IAC tax exemption (with its own separate conditions), easier winding up and easier public-procurement norms.
  3. Recognition is not incorporation, Udyam registration, a licence, funding approval or tax exemption. Each needs its own process.
  4. Funding links such as the Fund of Funds work through selected investment funds (AIFs). The startup still has to convince those funds.
  5. Use benefits that are relevant to your stage, and keep compliance and documents up to date.

3See it applied

Example

A DPIIT-recognised hardware startup may use IPR support for patent filing and procurement relaxation to approach government buyers.

Case lens

Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0

Government capital flows into SEBI-registered AIFs, which then invest in startups using their own appraisal. Recognition opens the door, but the startup still has to win the investor.

Think: Why does a Fund of Funds not give money directly to every recognised startup?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Assuming eligibility rules never change.
  • Treating recognition as funding approval.
  • Assuming tax benefits are automatic, when they need specific conditions and an application.

5Key terms

Startup India
The national initiative that recognises and supports eligible startups through DPIIT.
Section 80-IAC
The Income Tax Act provision offering an eligible recognised startup a tax holiday on profits, subject to separate certification and conditions.
Fund of Funds
A fund that invests in other investment funds, which in turn invest in startups.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Explain what Startup India recognition may enable and what it does not guarantee.

Recognition may enable access to self-certification, startup IPR support, Section 80-IAC tax exemption (subject to conditions), easier public-procurement norms, easier winding up and selected funding channels. It does not guarantee investment, a bank loan, tax relief, procurement orders, sector approvals, customers or commercial success.
