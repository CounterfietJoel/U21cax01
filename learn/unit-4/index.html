Unit IV · CO4 · Explain the methodology to start an enterprise systematically

Startup Opportunities and Process

Starting a venture is a sequence of progressively larger commitments. This unit covers how to find and frame the problem (design thinking and brainstorming), where today's opportunities come from (the new industrial revolution), how to turn an idea into a venture and choose its route, and the policy, registration, ecosystem, approval and tax steps that make it legal.

Remember two ideas. Learn first, commit progressively, so cheap and reversible tests come before leases, machines and large teams. Each approval is a separate decision, so incorporation, DPIIT recognition, Udyam, GST and sector licences never substitute for one another.

2-mark questions with model answers

Write your answer first. A 2-mark answer needs a precise definition or two clear points, not a paragraph.

Define design thinking.

Design thinking is a human-centred, iterative problem-solving approach in which a team understands users deeply, defines the real problem, generates alternatives, prototypes and tests them before committing to a solution.

List the five common stages of design thinking.

Empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test.

State the meaning of empathy in design thinking.

Empathy means understanding users' tasks, context, constraints, workarounds and feelings by observing and listening to them, rather than assuming what they need.

Define a problem statement.

A problem statement is a focused description of a target user, the situation, the unmet need or barrier, its consequence and the supporting evidence, written without assuming a solution.

State the meaning of a prototype.

A prototype is a low-cost, early representation of a product or service, such as a sketch, mock-up, model or working rig, that makes an idea testable.

State the purpose of prototype testing.

Prototype testing compares observed user behaviour and technical performance against decision criteria so the team can decide whether to continue, change, pause or stop before committing more resources.

Define iteration in design thinking.

Iteration is repeating and refining the stages using evidence from tests. The team may return to empathise, define or ideate whenever results show a gap.

State the meaning of ideation.

Ideation is the stage of design thinking in which a team generates, combines and develops many possible solutions to a defined problem before selecting concepts to prototype.

Define divergent thinking.

Divergent thinking widens the range of possibilities by producing many varied ideas without immediate judgement, as in brainstorming.

Define convergent thinking.

Convergent thinking narrows many options to a few by applying criteria and evidence, such as must-have screens or weighted scoring.

List two features of Industry 4.0.

(1) Connected cyber-physical systems, in which machines with sensors communicate over networks. (2) Data-driven automation using AI and analytics, for example predictive maintenance. Robotics, additive manufacturing and digital twins are also valid answers.

State the meaning of a controlled market pilot.

A controlled market pilot is a limited, time-bound trial with a defined customer, scope, baseline, acceptance thresholds, safety controls, data plan and decision date, used to test the offering under real conditions before scaling.

Define a small business.

A small business is an independently owned and managed enterprise of limited size, usually serving local customers, that aims for stable income and steady growth rather than rapid scaling.

State the meaning of a scalable startup.

A scalable startup is a young venture searching for a repeatable, scalable business model whose revenue can grow much faster than its cost, often with equity funding.

State the meaning of a social enterprise.

A social enterprise is a venture whose main aim is social or environmental impact, but which sustains itself through earned revenue rather than relying only on donations.

State the purpose of DPIIT startup recognition.

DPIIT recognition gives an eligible entity startup status under the Startup India framework. Subject to separate conditions, this can open access to benefits such as self-certification, IPR fast-tracking, Section 80-IAC tax exemption, easier public procurement and easier winding up. It does not guarantee funding or customers.

Define incorporation.

Incorporation is the legal process of registering a company or LLP with the relevant authority (the Registrar of Companies through MCA in India), creating a separate legal entity with its own rights, liabilities and perpetual succession.

Define intellectual property.

Intellectual property is a legally protectable creation of the mind, such as an invention (patent), the visual design of an article, original works including code (copyright), a brand (trademark) or confidential know-how (trade secret).

Define a business incubator.

A business incubator is an organisation, often hosted by a college or agency, that supports early-stage ventures with workspace, mentoring, shared facilities, networks, training and sometimes seed funding, to improve their chances of survival.

State the meaning of a sector-specific approval.

A sector-specific approval is a licence, registration or clearance required because of the particular product or activity, beyond general entity and tax registration. Examples are FSSAI for food businesses, drug or medical-device approvals for health products and pollution consent for certain manufacturing.

14-mark questions

Plan before you write: an introduction, the points below as headings with explanation and an example, then a short conclusion. Open a question to see the points to cover.

1Using the textbook flowchart, explain the sequence for setting up a small enterprise. State which registrations and clearances require verification from current official sources.

Points to cover

  • Charantimath Fig. 5.1 sequence is selection of project (product or service, location, feasibility, business plan, project profile) → decide the constitution → registration → clearances → land or shed → project report → finance → plant and machinery → implement.
  • Explain the purpose of each step briefly.
  • Constitution choices are proprietorship, partnership, LLP or company.
  • Registrations to verify are incorporation, Udyam, GST, shop and establishment and labour.
  • Clearances to verify are pollution consent, factory licence, fire, power and sector licences (via NSWS and state portals).
  • Rules and thresholds change, so check them on official portals before acting.

2Explain the empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test stages of design thinking.

Points to cover

  • Design thinking is human-centred and iterative.
  • Empathise — observe and interview users, and map the journey.
  • Define — write a problem statement naming user, need, consequence and evidence.
  • Ideate — diverge, then converge on testable concepts.
  • Prototype — build the minimum representation needed to learn.
  • Test — compare against thresholds and decide whether to continue, change, pause or stop, with an example such as the smart helmet or Krishigati.

Study: IV.1 Introduction to design thinking

3Describe brainstorming, SCAMPER, mind mapping, customer interviews and observation as methods for generating startup ideas.

Points to cover

  • Brainstorming is quantity before judgement, with a clear challenge, silent start, clustering and scoring.
  • SCAMPER is substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to another use, eliminate and reverse.
  • Mind mapping visually links problems, users, causes and solutions.
  • Customer interviews reveal motives, workarounds and priorities.
  • Observation shows what users actually do.
  • Separate generation from screening, with an example such as hostel water wastage.

4Explain startup opportunities associated with cyber-physical systems, robotics, additive manufacturing, AI and industrial data.

Points to cover

  • Industry 4.0 is the convergence of sensing, connectivity, computation and automation.
  • Cyber-physical systems enable monitoring and control retrofits for legacy machines.
  • Robotics brings safer work and cobot cells for SMEs.
  • Additive manufacturing enables spares on demand, jigs and fixtures, and small batches.
  • AI and industrial data enable predictive maintenance, defect detection and traceability.
  • The opportunity is the measurable outcome, while affordability, service and cybersecurity decide adoption.

Study: IV.3 The new industrial revolution

5Explain the features of a small business, scalable startup, social enterprise, franchise, consulting practice and technology-licensing venture.

Points to cover

  • Small business — local customers, stable income, low to moderate capital.
  • Scalable startup — a repeatable model, rapid growth, equity funding.
  • Social enterprise — impact plus sustainable revenue.
  • Franchise — a proven system, fees and royalties, brand restrictions.
  • Consulting — expertise-based, low capital, limited scalability.
  • Technology licensing — earns royalties from IP, is R&D-heavy and needs strong IP protection. Choose the model before the funding.

6Describe the legal and compliance categories relevant to a technology startup.

Points to cover

  • Entity and governance — incorporation, founder agreement and board.
  • Tax — PAN, GST, TDS and income tax filings.
  • Labour and premises — shop and establishment, labour registrations, local body.
  • IP — ownership, patents, copyright, trademarks and confidentiality.
  • Contracts and data — customer and vendor contracts, privacy and cybersecurity.
  • Sector, quality and trade — sector licences, standards and import-export. Keep an applicability register.

7Explain why startup recognition, MSME registration or incubator admission is not proof of customer demand.

Points to cover

  • Recognition and registration confirm eligibility and status, not demand.
  • Incubator admission reflects a selection panel's view of potential, not customer behaviour.
  • Demand is proved by customer behaviour such as paid pilots, repeat orders and retention.
  • Policy is an enabler, not a substitute for customer value.
  • Ventures built to collect grants fail when support ends.
  • Pair recognition with validation evidence.

Study: IV.6 Startup policy and opportunities

8Explain how incubators, laboratories, mentors, early customers, financiers and public agencies support a venture at different stages.

Points to cover

  • Idea stage — mentors and incubator pre-programmes.
  • Prototype stage — laboratories and technical institutions for testing.
  • Pilot stage — early customers and incubator seed support.
  • Enterprise setup — DIC, MSME bodies and banks.
  • Scale — angels, VC and public procurement.
  • Match each stage's gap to the right resource, and know each actor's limitations.

Study: IV.8 Startup ecosystem and legal environment

9(i) Explain the weaknesses of building an advanced product before understanding users.(ii) Describe how the design-thinking stages correct this technology-first approach.

Points to cover

  • The team risks solving the wrong problem or over-engineering, raising cost.
  • Users may not adopt it, and money and time are spent on irreversible commitments.
  • Empathise uncovers real needs and context.
  • Define frames the problem without assuming a solution.
  • Ideate and prototype explore alternatives cheaply.
  • Test against thresholds and iterate on the evidence.

Study: IV.1 Introduction to design thinking, IV.4 Business startup and ideation

10(i) Explain the features of a private limited company and an LLP.(ii) Describe two financing contexts in which the suitability of these forms differs.

Points to cover

  • Private limited company — Companies Act 2013, separate entity, limited liability, shares, 2 to 200 members, higher compliance.
  • LLP — LLP Act 2008, separate entity, limited liability, partners and designated partners, lighter compliance, no equity shares.
  • Context 1 — angel or VC equity, which favours a private limited company because shares can be issued.
  • Context 2 — a bootstrapped professional or consulting firm, where an LLP offers flexibility with lower compliance.
  • Both can apply for DPIIT recognition if eligible.
  • Conclude by matching the form to the funding route.

Study: IV.7 Registration and legal process of startups, IV.5 Venture choices

11(i) Describe the categories of approvals that may apply to a food-product startup.(ii) Explain why entity registration does not replace sector, local, tax, labour and quality obligations.

Points to cover

  • Food-business registration or licence (FSSAI), labelling and packaging.
  • Premises and local trade licence, plus testing and shelf-life records.
  • GST, labour registrations and fire safety where applicable.
  • Incorporation creates the entity but does not authorise the product, premises or activity.
  • Each approval is a separate decision by a different authority.
  • Keep an applicability register and verify on official portals.

12(i) Explain the purpose and components of a controlled pilot for an industrial sensing product.(ii) Describe suitable technical, user, business, safety and learning metrics.

Points to cover

  • The purpose is to test the offering under real conditions before scaling, with a clear decision date.
  • Components are customer and context, scope, duration, responsibilities, baseline, thresholds, safety controls, data plan, support and commercial terms.
  • Technical metrics — uptime and false-alarm rate.
  • User metrics — dashboard use per shift.
  • Business metrics — downtime saved and willingness to sign a paid contract.
  • Safety and learning — no unsafe interventions, plus a recorded list of failure modes.

Study: IV.4 Business startup and ideation

13(i) Explain the relationship among patent review, prototype disclosure, investor pitching and customer trials.(ii) Describe a practical IP and confidentiality sequence.

Points to cover

  • Public disclosure before filing can destroy novelty.
  • Pitches and trials are disclosures, so protection decisions must come first.
  • Sequence step 1 — confirm ownership of all contributions.
  • Step 2 — document the invention and prior art, then run a preliminary search.
  • Step 3 — use NDAs and access controls, and choose the route (patent, design, copyright, trademark, trade secret or contract).
  • Step 4 — file before uncontrolled disclosure, then pitch and run trials with proportionate protection.

Study: IV.7 Registration and legal process of startups

14(i) Explain how eligibility, stage, need, obligation, timing and evidence are used to screen a government-policy benefit.(ii) Describe a scheme-screening matrix using these headings.

Points to cover

  • Eligibility — entity, age, turnover, sector and location.
  • Stage — idea, prototype, pilot, MSME or scale.
  • Need — whether it addresses the actual constraint.
  • Obligation — contribution, milestones, reporting, equity or repayment.
  • Timing and evidence — window, disbursal timing, documents and official source date.
  • Present a matrix with schemes as rows and headings as columns, marking any scheme with an unclear condition as unverified.

Study: IV.6 Startup policy and opportunities

15(i) Explain the risks of incorporating, renting facilities and purchasing machinery before validating demand.(ii) Describe how staged commitments reduce these risks.

Points to cover

  • Fixed costs and compliance start before revenue, so cash burns quickly.
  • Leases and machinery are hard to reverse and may not fit the validated need.
  • A pivot becomes expensive or impossible.
  • Staging means interviews, prototypes and pilots come first because they are cheap and reversible.
  • Incorporate when contracts, IP or funding require it, and buy machinery once orders justify it.
  • Run legal, technical and commercial workstreams in parallel.
