IV.1 Introduction to design thinking

You should be able to: Explain design thinking for startup opportunity discovery.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 42
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Design thinking is a human-centred problem-solving approach that helps founders understand users deeply before fixing a solution. Its five stages are empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test. It is iterative, so evidence can send the team back to any earlier stage, and returning is learning, not failure.

  1. It is most useful when the problem is complex, user behaviour matters or the first solution is uncertain.
  2. It corrects the technology-first error. Instead of 'What can we build?' it asks what users need, why the problem exists and what they will actually adopt.
  3. A strong problem statement names the user, the situation, the barrier or unmet need, its consequence and the evidence, without naming a solution. 'Lab technicians need a reliable way to cut sample waiting time because manual hand-offs create queues and missed priority cases' is stronger than 'the lab needs an app'.
  4. A prototype is the minimum representation needed to learn. A good early prototype tests the riskiest assumption cheaply.
  5. Human-centred does not mean technically weak. It means technical decisions are tied to real use, safety and value.

2Five stages

  • EmpathiseObserve and listen to users in their context.
  • DefineTurn observations into a focused problem statement.
  • IdeateGenerate many solutions, then select testable concepts.
  • PrototypeBuild a low-cost representation to learn from.
  • TestCompare results with criteria, then continue, change, pause or stop.
Each stage produces evidence, and each has a typical error
StageEvidence producedCommon error
EmpathiseNeeds, workarounds and the user's languageAsking only whether users like the proposed solution
DefineA focused problem statementWriting the preferred technology into the problem
IdeateAlternative concepts and the reason for the selectionCriticising early or selecting by seniority
PrototypeVisible behaviour or performanceTreating prototype polish as market evidence
TestA continue, change, pause or stop decisionMoving the threshold after seeing the result

3See it applied

Example

Before designing a smart helmet, a team should observe riders, delivery workers, traffic conditions, comfort issues, charging behaviour and price sensitivity.

Case lens

Krishigati

Begin with farmer observation, define the operational pain points, prototype a field-usable tool and test for affordability and adoption.

Think: What would Krishigati's team look for during the empathise stage on a small farm?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Building an advanced product before understanding users.
  • Treating the stages as a one-way sequence.
  • Calling user praise for a concept 'validation'.

5Key terms

Design thinking
A human-centred, iterative approach that moves through empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test.
Empathy
Understanding users' tasks, constraints, workarounds and emotions through observation and listening.
Prototype
A low-cost representation of an idea, built to test an assumption.
Iteration
Repeating and revising stages based on evidence from testing.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define design thinking.

Design thinking is a human-centred, iterative problem-solving approach in which a team understands users deeply, defines the real problem, generates alternatives, prototypes and tests them before committing to a solution.

List the five common stages of design thinking.

Empathise, define, ideate, prototype and test.

State the meaning of empathy in design thinking.

Empathy means understanding users' tasks, context, constraints, workarounds and feelings by observing and listening to them, rather than assuming what they need.

Define a problem statement.

A problem statement is a focused description of a target user, the situation, the unmet need or barrier, its consequence and the supporting evidence, written without assuming a solution.

State the meaning of a prototype.

A prototype is a low-cost, early representation of a product or service, such as a sketch, mock-up, model or working rig, that makes an idea testable.

State the purpose of prototype testing.

Prototype testing compares observed user behaviour and technical performance against decision criteria so the team can decide whether to continue, change, pause or stop before committing more resources.

Define iteration in design thinking.

Iteration is repeating and refining the stages using evidence from tests. The team may return to empathise, define or ideate whenever results show a gap.
