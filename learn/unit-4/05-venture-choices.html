IV.5 Venture choices

You should be able to: Compare venture choices available to entrepreneurs.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 45
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Entrepreneurs can choose among a small business, scalable startup, social enterprise, family business, franchise, consulting practice, manufacturing unit, digital platform, technology licensing or intrapreneurship. The right choice depends on founder goals, risk tolerance, capital need, growth ambition, technical complexity, legal exposure and market opportunity.

  1. Not only VC-funded startups are valuable. A profitable small manufacturing or service business is an excellent entrepreneurial outcome.
  2. Small business — stable income and local customers. Scalable startup — searches for a repeatable model that can grow fast. Social enterprise — impact with sustainable revenue.
  3. Franchise — a proven system with brand restrictions. Technology licensing — earns royalties from IP without building the full business. Consulting — sells expertise, with low capital needs but limited scalability.
  4. Choose the model before the funding. A model that cannot scale fast should not take VC money.
  5. Social ventures still need financial discipline.

2Routes compared

  • Small businessStable income and local customers.
  • Scalable startupRapid growth and a repeatable model.
  • Social enterpriseImpact with sustainable revenue.
  • FranchiseA proven system with brand restrictions.
  • Technology ventureInnovation-led, often needing IP and R&D.

3See it applied

Example

A robotics student can choose a training service, a component manufacturing unit, a SaaS maintenance platform or a deep-tech product company, depending on resources and ambition.

Case lens

Quixy and Autocracy Machinery

Quixy is a scalable software startup with low capital intensity. Autocracy Machinery is a manufacturing venture with tooling, inventory and field service. Both are valid, but they need different capital, teams and funding.

Think: Which of the two is better suited to bank term-loan finance, and why?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Choosing the funding before choosing the model.
  • Picking a high-growth model without the market and team capacity for it.
  • Assuming social-impact ventures do not need financial discipline.

5Key terms

Small business
An independently owned enterprise serving mainly local customers, aiming for stable income rather than rapid scale.
Scalable startup
A young venture searching for a repeatable business model that can grow revenue much faster than cost.
Social enterprise
A venture whose primary aim is social or environmental impact, financed sustainably through earned revenue.
Technology licensing
Granting another firm the right to use your IP in exchange for fees or royalties.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

Define a small business.

A small business is an independently owned and managed enterprise of limited size, usually serving local customers, that aims for stable income and steady growth rather than rapid scaling.

State the meaning of a scalable startup.

A scalable startup is a young venture searching for a repeatable, scalable business model whose revenue can grow much faster than its cost, often with equity funding.

State the meaning of a social enterprise.

A social enterprise is a venture whose main aim is social or environmental impact, but which sustains itself through earned revenue rather than relying only on donations.
