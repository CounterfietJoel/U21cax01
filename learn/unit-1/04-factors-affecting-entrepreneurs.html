I.4 Factors affecting entrepreneurs

You should be able to: Identify factors influencing entrepreneurial success.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 8
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Entrepreneurs are shaped by their personal qualities and by the environment around them. The textbook groups growth factors into economic, social, psychological and government factors. These can also be split into internal factors (inside the founder and team) and external factors (in the environment).

  1. Economic factors are capital, skilled labour, raw materials, infrastructure, technology and market demand.
  2. Social factors are family support, community values, role models, respect for business, social networks and social security.
  3. Psychological factors are need for achievement, self-confidence, risk-taking ability, persistence and willingness to learn.
  4. Government factors are industrial policy, registration systems, tax rules, subsidies, incubation, training, procurement access and ease of approvals.
  5. Economic factors are often contrasted with non-economic (social, psychological and political) factors. Exams often ask for both.

2Four factor groups

  • EconomicCapital, labour, raw material, market, technology and infrastructure.
  • SocialValues, status of business, role models, family support and networks.
  • PsychologicalAchievement motivation, confidence, resilience and risk attitude.
  • GovernmentPolicy, regulation, incentives, training and institutional support.
Internal versus external factors
TypeExamples
Internal (founder and team)Skills, motivation, confidence, risk attitude, team capability, execution discipline
External (environment)Finance access, market demand, infrastructure, regulation, social attitudes, government support

3See it applied

Example

A drone service startup for agriculture needs skilled pilots and working capital (economic), farmer trust (social), confidence to handle seasonal demand (psychological) and DGCA awareness (government).

Case lens

Greenviro Global, Coimbatore

Greenviro depends on economic factors (a steady coconut-waste supply and working capital), social factors (local acceptance of briquettes as a fuel), psychological factors (the founder persisting with an unfamiliar product) and government factors (environmental rules and any available support for waste-to-energy units).

Think: Which one factor would most limit Greenviro if it expanded to another district?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Assuming a good idea succeeds even when finance, market or regulation is ignored.
  • Forgetting that family and social pressure can either support or discourage entrepreneurship.
  • Believing a government scheme helps automatically. It helps only when the founder understands eligibility and compliance.

5Key terms

Economic factors
Material conditions such as capital, labour, raw materials, infrastructure and market demand that enable or limit a venture.
Non-economic factors
Social, psychological and political or government conditions that influence entrepreneurship.
Need for achievement
A psychological drive to accomplish challenging goals and improve performance.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

List two internal factors affecting entrepreneurial growth.

(1) The founder's motivation and need for achievement, and (2) the team's skills and capability. Risk attitude, confidence and execution discipline are also acceptable.

List two external factors affecting entrepreneurial growth.

(1) Access to finance, and (2) market demand. Infrastructure, government policy and regulation, and social attitudes are also acceptable.

Name any two motives for becoming an entrepreneur.

(1) Achievement and independence, being one's own boss, and (2) financial reward or wealth creation. Applying one's knowledge, solving a meaningful problem, social impact and limited employment options are also valid.
