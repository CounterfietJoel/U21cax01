I.2 Characteristics of entrepreneurship

You should be able to: Discuss major entrepreneurial characteristics.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 6
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Entrepreneurship requires creativity, innovation, initiative, leadership, persistence, problem-solving ability, commitment and willingness to take calculated risks. These work together. A good entrepreneur combines imagination with disciplined execution.

  1. Creativity generates possible ideas. Innovation turns selected ideas into practical applications. Leadership mobilises people and resources around the opportunity.
  2. Commitment and persistence matter because early ventures face delays, rejection, shortage of funds and repeated redesign.
  3. Engineering founders also need technical judgement. Can the product be manufactured, serviced, scaled, certified and protected from easy imitation?
  4. Entrepreneurial competency combines knowledge, skill, behaviour and judgement.
  5. Confidence without evidence becomes overconfidence. Persistence without learning becomes escalation of commitment.

2Core entrepreneurial characteristics

  • Creative thinkingAbility to generate alternatives instead of accepting the first answer.
  • Achievement motivationA strong desire to build, improve and excel.
  • Decision-makingChoosing well with incomplete information.
  • ResourcefulnessDoing more with limited resources.
  • Calculated risk-takingTaking risks in measured steps, not gambling.
  • EthicsBuilding trust with customers, investors, workers and society.

3See it applied

Example

A mechatronics team building an automated seed-sowing robot must show creativity in mechanism design, persistence during field failures and honesty when reporting actual field performance.

Case lens

Greenviro Global, Coimbatore

Turning coconut waste into charcoal and briquettes needed creativity (seeing value in waste), initiative (acting on a local opportunity) and persistence (building a revenue model around it).

Think: Name two characteristics from the framework that this venture clearly needed, and say why.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Letting confidence become overconfidence.
  • Taking careless risks instead of calculated ones.
  • Believing technical excellence alone is enough without understanding the market.

5Key terms

Entrepreneurial competency
The combination of knowledge, skill, behaviour and judgement that lets a person create and run a venture.
Innovation
The practical introduction of a new or significantly improved solution that creates value.
Creativity
The ability to generate new and useful ideas or alternatives.

6Quick check

Pick an answer to see the explanation. Get all of them right to mark this topic as revised.

  1. 1What is the difference between creativity and innovation?

    Creativity produces possibilities. Innovation is the practical introduction of a selected idea so that it creates value.

  2. 2A founder keeps investing in a product for two years although every customer test fails. What is this?

    Persistence is valuable only when it is combined with learning. Ignoring repeated evidence is escalation of commitment.

  3. 3Why do engineering founders need technical judgement in addition to creativity?

    A product that cannot be manufactured, serviced, scaled or certified cannot become a sustainable venture, however creative it is.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

Answer in your own words first, then open the model answer to compare.

List any two characteristics of a successful entrepreneur.

Any two, for example (1) creativity and innovation, the ability to generate ideas and turn them into practical value, and (2) calculated risk-taking, acting under uncertainty in measured steps. Persistence, leadership, initiative and ethics are also acceptable.

Define entrepreneurial competency.

Entrepreneurial competency is the combination of knowledge, skills, behaviour and judgement that enables a person to recognise opportunities, mobilise resources and build a venture. Examples include opportunity recognition, customer orientation, persistence and financial awareness.

Name two ethical responsibilities of an entrepreneur.

(1) Report product performance and safety results honestly, and (2) keep fair commitments to customers, workers and investors. Protecting customer data and the environment are also valid answers.
