I.9 Role of entrepreneurship in economic development

You should be able to: Discuss the economic contribution of entrepreneurship.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 12
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

Entrepreneurship drives economic development by mobilising savings, forming capital, creating employment, improving productivity, introducing innovation, reducing regional imbalance, developing exports and improving living standards. Entrepreneurs bring land, labour, capital, technology and knowledge together to produce goods and services.

  1. In developing economies, large employers alone cannot absorb all skilled youth, so entrepreneurship is an important source of jobs.
  2. Job creation includes direct jobs and indirect jobs in supply chains, logistics and services.
  3. Entrepreneurs introduce competition, commercialise knowledge and develop local supplier networks.
  4. Inclusive development happens when entrepreneurship reaches rural areas, women, first-generation founders, social enterprises and local clusters.
  5. Creative destruction happens when a superior product, process or business model draws resources away from older arrangements. The gain is real, but displaced workers and transition costs need responsible attention.

2Five contributions (exam headings)

  • Capital formationMobilising savings into investment and reinvestment.
  • Employment generationDirect and indirect jobs.
  • InnovationNew products, processes and markets that raise productivity.
  • Balanced regional developmentLocal enterprises and clusters outside big cities.
  • Exports and import substitutionEarning foreign exchange and reducing import dependence.

3See it applied

Example

A food-processing startup in a semi-urban area can create farmer linkages, packaging jobs, logistics demand, retail revenue and local tax contribution.

Case lens

India startup ecosystem snapshot

12 lakh-plus self-reported jobs and startups in 670 districts by end-2023 show both employment generation and regional spread.

Think: Which two contributions in the framework does this data support most directly?

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Valuing only growth. Entrepreneurship must also be sustainable.
  • Counting only factory jobs and ignoring supply-chain and service jobs.
  • Forgetting ethics, safety and environmental responsibility when discussing development.

5Key terms

Capital formation
Converting savings into productive investment such as plant, equipment and infrastructure.
Creative destruction
The process by which superior products, processes or business models replace older ones and reallocate resources.
Import substitution
Producing domestically what was previously imported.

7Exam practice: 2-mark questions

List two contributions of entrepreneurship to economic development.

(1) Employment generation, through direct and indirect jobs, and (2) capital formation, by mobilising savings into productive investment. Innovation, balanced regional development and export promotion are also acceptable.
