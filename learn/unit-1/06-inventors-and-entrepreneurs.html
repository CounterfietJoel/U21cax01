I.6 Inventors and entrepreneurs

You should be able to: Differentiate between inventors and entrepreneurs.

  • 📖 Read first: U21CAX01 Study Material, p. 10
  • ⏱ About 10 minutes to revise

1In a nutshell

An inventor creates a new idea, device, process or technical solution. An entrepreneur converts an idea into value by organising resources, building a delivery system, reaching customers and sustaining the venture. The bridge between the two is validation.

  1. Many inventors stop at the prototype or patent stage. Many entrepreneurs commercialise, adapt or improve ideas they did not invent.
  2. An invention is a novel technical creation. An innovation is a new or improved solution that is implemented and produces value.
  3. The same invention can support several opportunities, because different customers value different outcomes such as safety, speed, cost or compliance.
  4. The inventor-to-entrepreneur shift is from mechanism-centred thinking to problem-and-system thinking, which covers user behaviour, manufacturing, service, certification, IP, channel, price and cash timing.
  5. The conversion point is moving from proof of concept to proof of value.

2Inventor versus entrepreneur

  • Inventor focusNovelty, mechanism, design and the technical solution.
  • Entrepreneur focusCustomer, cost, revenue, risk, team and scale.
  • Shared areasInnovation, problem-solving, intellectual property and persistence.
  • Conversion pointProof of concept becomes proof of value.

3See it applied

Example

A student who designs a new low-cost water-level sensor is an inventor. The student becomes an entrepreneur when the sensor is packaged, priced, tested in homes or farms and supported with installation and service.

Case lens

Breathe Applied Sciences

The technical core, converting CO₂ to methanol, is the invention. The entrepreneurial challenge is turning that science into a usable industrial solution with clear cost, process, customer and regulatory answers.

Think: List two questions an investor would ask Breathe that an inventor alone might not answer.

4Avoid these mistakes

  • Assuming a patent guarantees market success.
  • Assuming a prototype proves willingness to pay.
  • Protecting technical quality but neglecting customer trust.

5Key terms

Invention
A novel technical creation such as a new device, process or solution.
Proof of concept
Evidence that a technical idea can work.
Proof of value
Evidence that customers gain enough value to adopt and pay for the solution.
